=== flash, default | How is HIV transmitted? | 8.7s
HIV can be transmitted through:
- Sexual contact, such as vaginal, anal, or oral sex
- Exchange of blood and bodily fluids
- Sharing needles, syringes, or skin-piercing instruments like those used for tattooing or ear piercing
- Passing from a mother to her baby during pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding

HIV is not transmitted through air, water, saliva, sweat, tears, closed-mouth kissing, insects, pets, or sharing toilets, food, or drinks.

Please visit a healthcare worker at a local health facility for personalized advice and medical care.

=== flash, default | What are the symptoms of HPV? | 9.0s
Hello! Most people with HPV do not show symptoms right away. Often, a person only finds out they have HPV when health problems appear.

Symptoms and conditions caused by HPV include:
- Genital warts, which can stay the same, grow, or go away
- Abnormal Pap test results in women during cervical screening
- Cancers, such as cervical, vulvar, vaginal, penile, anal, or back of the throat cancer, which can develop years later

Because HPV often shows no early signs, please visit a health facility to get proper guidance and professional care for your personal medical decisions.

=== flash, default | How can I protect myself from getting STIs? | 9.3s
Hello! You can protect yourself from STIs in the following ways:

- Delaying sexual activity or abstaining from sex until in a stable relationship.
- Learning how to use condoms properly before becoming sexually active.
- Using condoms during sex, which effectively reduces STI risk.
- Limiting your number of sexual partners and sticking to one partner.
- Adult voluntary medical male circumcision.
- Informing your partner if you have an STI so they can also get treated, preventing re-infection.

Please consult a qualified healthcare professional or visit a health facility for personal medical advice and care.

=== flash, default | How is gonorrhoea treated? | 8.5s
Gonorrhoea is typically treated using dual therapy, which combines antibiotics like Ceftriaxone and Azithromycin, or Cefixime and Azithromycin. In certain cases where local resistance data allows, single therapy using Ceftriaxone or Cefixime may be prescribed. Treatment options might also include medicines for anaerobes, such as Metronidazole, or treatment for co-infections.

It is important to remember that the right medicine and correct dose must always come directly from a health worker.

Please visit a health facility to speak with a healthcare professional for personal medical care and proper treatment.
